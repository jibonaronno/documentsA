I am planning to make a Mobile app, that will show virtual flash cards of Hangul and English sentences. The cards are categorised by LevelA, LevelB, LevelC etc. I need a floating menu bar at left side. What is the method to bring up the menubar to select a level or a card under a level ? WHat is the name of the Icon that brings up the menu bar ?



The pattern you’re describing is usually called a navigation drawer: a panel that slides in from the left side of the screen.

The icon that opens it is commonly called the hamburger menu icon—three horizontal lines: ☰.

For your flashcard app, a simple flow would be:

The user taps ☰ in the top-left corner.
The navigation drawer slides in from the left.
The drawer lists Level A, Level B, Level C, etc.
Tapping a level expands its card list. This expandable pattern is called an accordion.
Tapping a card closes the drawer and displays that flashcard.

If each level has many cards, selecting a level should open a separate card-list screen rather than expanding a very long list inside the drawer.

4. Adding user flashcards
Provide an Add flashcard option in the drawer.

The form should contain:

Field	
Requirement

Level/group	Required; select an existing level
English sentence	Required
Hangul sentence	Required
Short title	Optional
Order number	Automatically append to the end of the level, unless the user chooses another position
Users should also be able to edit or delete their own cards.

Scope: User-added cards are stored locally on that device. Sharing them with other users or including them in everyone’s app would require a separate submission and publishing feature.

5. Local database
A local database such as SQLite is suitable and works offline.

A practical structure is:

Table	Main fields	
Purpose

Levels	ID, name, display order	Stores Level A, Level B, Level C, etc.
Flashcards	ID, level ID, order number, title, English sentence, Hangul sentence, source	Stores built-in and user-created cards
App state	Last-viewed card ID	Restores the previous study position
Use a stable, unique card ID, rather than the order number, to identify each card. Order numbers may change when cards are inserted or rearranged.

The source field distinguishes built-in cards from user-created cards.

Updating the database safely
When an app update includes new or corrected built-in cards:

Merge those changes into the existing database using stable card IDs.
Preserve all user-created cards.
Do not replace the entire installed database with a new bundled copy.
If the last-viewed card no longer exists, open a valid fallback card.
Local storage generally survives normal app updates, but it may be lost if the user uninstalls the app or clears its data. An export/import feature would provide a useful backup option.

6. Ad area
Your requested bottom-quarter reservation can be implemented as a fixed layout area.

However, the ad itself should use a supported size from the chosen ad network. Do not stretch a banner to fill the entire quarter-screen area. Place the supported ad inside that space, respecting safe areas and keeping it separate from interactive controls.

Also:

Do not overlay ads on flashcard content.
Keep the layout stable while ads load.
Do not let ads intercept flashcard swipes.
Hide or appropriately cover the ad when the modal drawer is open so it cannot interfere with menu interaction.
A quarter of the screen is a substantial amount of space, so test that both sentences remain readable on smaller phones and with larger accessibility text settings.

7. Sentence data you can provide later
You can send your sentences in this format:

Level	Order	English sentence	Hangul sentence	
Optional title

Level A	1	Your English sentence	Your Hangul sentence	Greetings
Level A	2	Your English sentence	Your Hangul sentence	Introductions
Level B	1	Your English sentence	Your Hangul sentence	Everyday phrases
Once you provide the sentences, they can be organized into database tables or an importable dataset with level assignments and card ordering.
